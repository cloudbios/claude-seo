# Industry planning options

Use the `industry` value from `input.json` to select one section. These are
decision prompts derived from the upstream planning templates, not required
pages or a claim about every business in that industry. Omit an option when
the business cannot support it. Resolve material uncertainty in the plan.

## generic

Start with the audience's task and the business's actual offer. Make the core
offer, evidence of suitability and next action understandable before adding
a resource library. Group only known products/services. Ask which questions
customers need answered and who can maintain each proposed page. Use this
section when a more specific industry is not supported by the supplied facts.

## saas

Distinguish product discovery, evaluation and successful use. Consider pages
for documented capabilities, relevant use cases, actual integrations, pricing
explanations and support documentation. Comparisons require evidence and fair
scope; case studies need real customer permission and outcomes. Do not invent
integrations, security certifications, savings or enterprise capabilities.
Plan ownership for keeping product claims aligned with releases.

## local-service

Help customers determine whether the business serves their need and area,
what the process involves and how to contact it. Plan service/location content
only where actual operations and useful local information support it; do not
generate a grid of city pages from keywords. Respect service-area businesses
that do not publish a street address. Treat listing ownership, hours, reviews
and local proof as facts to verify, not assumed assets. Assign maintenance of
service availability and contact details.

## ecommerce

Separate navigation/discovery from product evaluation and purchase support.
Consider known category relationships, product attributes, variants, stock
and policy questions. Buying guides should connect to actual products and
answer audience decisions. Plan who maintains changing product information,
shipping and returns. Do not invent stock, prices, certifications, reviews or
merchant eligibility; technical treatment of facets, duplicates and feeds
requires a separate assessment before implementation.

## publisher

Organize coverage around an editorial purpose and supported topic areas.
Consider author/source attribution, corrections, updates and the distinction
between reporting, analysis and opinion. Assign factual review and maintenance
before proposing publication volume. Topic archives should help readers find
coverage rather than create unsupported thin pages. Do not assume a news
organization, ad-funded model, specialist credentials or original research
unless supplied. Plan measurement around the declared audience/business goal.

## agency

Clarify the actual services, suitable clients, process and supported expertise.
Use work examples only with permission and evidence; separate the agency's
contribution from client outcomes. Consider service-to-example and
insight-to-service relationships where useful. Industry specialization pages
need demonstrated relevance, not a copied list of sectors. Assign review of
confidentiality, claims and portfolio freshness. Do not assume staffing,
accreditation, contract terms or guaranteed client results.
